# Sound

Use this reference for musical phrasing, memorable sound patterns, metre, and cadence.
Chapter numbers refer to *The Elements of Eloquence*.
Example conventions are defined in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#reference-conventions).

## 1. Alliteration

Repeat initial consonant sounds in nearby words or stressed syllables.
Sound matters more than spelling.
Use it for memorable names, phrases, and musical emphasis.

- "Full fathom five thy father lies." Shakespeare, *The Tempest*.
- "Curiosity killed the cat." Proverb.
- "Build better beginnings."

Choose words for their meaning first. Excess creates tongue twisters or accidental comedy.

## 21. Versification

Metre arranges recurring stresses. A foot is a recurring unit within that pattern.
This chapter examines verse forms and their effects.

### Feet

Capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

| Foot | Stress pattern | Two examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iamb | da DUM | be-LIEVE, re-TURN |
| Trochee | DUM da | GAR-den, SIL-ver |
| Anapaest | da da DUM | in the DARK, on the SHORE |
| Dactyl | DUM da da | HAP-pi-ly, TEN-der-ly |
| Amphibrach | da DUM da | re-MEM-ber, to-MOR-row |

Anapaest is also spelled *anapest*.
See the [Poetry Foundation's definition](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/education/glossary/anapest).

### Line length

- Trimeter: three feet.
- Tetrameter: four feet.
- Pentameter: five feet.

Combine the foot and count to name the metre.

- "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?" Shakespeare. Iambic pentameter.
- "She walks in beauty, like the night." Byron. Iambic tetrameter.
- "And his cohorts were gleaming in purple and gold." Byron. Anapaestic tetrameter.

### Verse forms

Common metre alternates iambic tetrameter and trimeter.
Ballad metre often alternates four beats with three, with greater flexibility.
These examples illustrate the alternation. A slash marks a line break.

- "The moon has found the windowpane / And silvered all the floor."
- "The rain has washed the road to glass / The lamps are burning low."

The *In Memoriam* stanza combines iambic tetrameter with ABBA rhyme.
Blank verse uses unrhymed iambic pentameter.
Limericks typically use anapaestic or amphibrachic rhythms with AABBA rhyme.

For prose, use cadence and sentence variation without forcing a fixed metre.
Metre permits substitutions. Treat the book's rhythmic preferences as guidance, not universal requirements.
Never add filler or distort word order merely to maintain a beat.

## 25. Assonance

Repeat vowel sounds in nearby words, especially stressed syllables.
This creates an internal echo without requiring full rhyme.

- "High as a kite." Common expression.
- "How now, brown cow?" Common phrase.
- "The pale rain veiled the lane."

The third example repeats the vowel heard in "day".
Pronunciation determines the pattern. Accent can change whether it works.
